giant steps

Coltrane's cadence in miniature: a plain ii–V–I is stretched through three key centres a major third apart, each reached by its own dominant. Press play to hear it, and watch the roots step down.

Why does the second chord — E♭7, the dominant of A♭ — follow the ii chord of C so smoothly? Three things are happening at once.

1It's almost a chromatic slide

Line up the notes of the two chords and nearly every voice moves up by a single half step — the whole chord planes upward:

Dm7E♭7motion
D root→E♭+1 semitone
F 3rd→G+2 semitones
A 5th→B♭+1 semitone
C 7th→D♭+1 semitone

three voices move a half step, one a whole step

In fact E♭7 is D7 shifted up a half step (D7 = D F♯ A C → E♭7 = E♭ G B♭ D♭), and Dm7 differs from D7 only in its third. So the ear hears a smooth chromatic shove upward with almost no friction — and smooth voice leading is what lets a "foreign" chord sound connected.

2A♭ is already in C's orbit

You might ask why C would ever wander toward A♭. But A♭ major is ♭VI of C — the chord you get by borrowing from C minor (modal mixture). ♭VI is one of the most common colour-key destinations in all of tonal music (deceptive cadences, Romantic harmony, film scores all lean on it). So tonicising A♭ isn't leaving home; it's visiting a neighbour C already owns — and E♭7 is simply the V7 that delivers you there.

3The ii chord is a springboard

A ii7 chord's job is to be pre-dominant tension — a launching pad that wants to fire into a dominant. Normally that's the home V (Dm7 → G7 → C), but a ii chord doesn't care which dominant it feeds, as long as the resolution convinces. Coltrane keeps the ordinary Dm7, then redirects the springboard toward E♭7 → A♭, delaying the arrival home. Dm7 becomes a pivot: pre-dominant in C, and the setup tension for the E♭7→A♭ cadence.

The big picture

That first swerve is the first "giant step." The whole passage is built so that C, A♭ and E divide the octave into three equal major thirds, each key reached by its own dominant 7th:

Dm7  →  E♭7 → A♭  →  B7 → E  →  G7 → C
ii7      V7    I      V7   I      V7   I
of C    └─→ A♭       └─→ E       └─→ C  (home)

Every dominant does the same trick — a smooth chromatic V pulling you a major third down to the next tonic — until G7 finally lands you home.

In one sentence: Dm7 is a pre-dominant springboard, E♭7 is the V of C's close chromatic-mediant relative A♭ (♭VI), and the two chords are joined by a near-parallel half-step slide — so it's smooth on the surface and logical underneath.
